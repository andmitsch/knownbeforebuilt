Four talks

Each tailored to the audience and industry.

  1. Abstract

    In established companies, the decision to build is usually made long before anyone has evidence that it should be. Roadmaps fill with client requests, executive conviction and competitive gap-fills, and the cost of a wrong bet hides below the waterline: years of maintenance, capacity pulled from the core business, and quiet write-offs. Drawing on his book Known Before Built, Andreas shows how leadership teams move from belief to evidence before committing serious money, using observed customer behavior, cheap assumption tests and pilots designed to end in a clear decision. He closes with the question every product leader hears, “Why can’t we just ship it?”, and how to answer it with a number.

    Download one-page PDF

    Takeaways

    1. Why the build cost is the cheapest part of a wrong decision, and the four cost layers (implementation, carrying, displacement, correction) that never appear in the business case.
    2. A staged path from first signal to investment decision, with clear decision rights at each gate: continue, hold, pivot or kill.
    3. How to answer “Why can’t we just ship it?” with a number instead of an opinion.

    Audience

    Executive teams, CEOs and COOs of industrial, consulting and professional-services firms, CPOs, heads of innovation and transformation, investment committees.

  2. Abstract

    Boards are funding AI on a simple promise: engineers ship faster, so the business grows faster. The first half may come true. The second does not follow. In a 2025 randomized trial by METR, experienced developers using AI tools took 19% longer on real tasks, yet believed they had been 20% faster. And where speed is real, it multiplies whatever enters the pipeline: a backlog of sales requests and stakeholder wishes becomes more features, shipped sooner, maintained forever. Andreas argues that the bottleneck was never engineering speed but deciding what is worth building, and shows what leaders must change so AI speed turns into outcomes.

    Download one-page PDF

    Takeaways

    1. Why output metrics (velocity, commits, features shipped) mislead boards about AI returns, and which outcome measures to track instead.
    2. The electric-motor lesson from economist Paul David: gains come from redesigning the system around a tool, not from the tool.
    3. Why AI removes engineering as product’s alibi, and what that means for accountability in the leadership team.

    Audience

    CEOs, CTOs, CPOs, CFOs and boards sponsoring AI programs; main stages at AI, digital transformation and industry conferences.

    Lengths

    Fireside

  3. Abstract

    Most customer research asks people what they think — would they use it, would they pay — and treats the answers as evidence. They are not. People are unreliable reporters of their own behavior: when Porter and Nohria tracked 27 CEOs minute by minute, their time went somewhere very different from where they believed it went. This session teaches research built on past and observed behavior: interviews that surface real workflows, workarounds and spend; how to tell normalized pain from complaints nobody would pay to fix; and how to define, before the conversation, which behaviors would confirm or end a hypothesis. Participants leave with question patterns they can use the next morning. The session is the hands-on companion to Known before built.

    Download one-page PDF

    Takeaways

    1. The evidence hierarchy: reported behavior beats opinion, and observed behavior beats both.
    2. Interview question patterns that pull out past behavior, workarounds and spend instead of predictions.
    3. How to set confirming and disconfirming behaviors before the interview, so findings cannot be bent to fit the plan.

    Audience

    Product managers, UX and market researchers, innovation and venture-building teams, consultants moving into product roles, and the leaders who sign off on their research.

    Lengths

    Workshop
    90 min or half day
    Guest lecture
    60–90 min

  4. Abstract

    In many companies, growth means hunting small incremental uplifts everywhere but the product itself. Onboarding, activation, adoption, retention, churn, expansion often have no clear owner once a product has reached a market. Andreas has spent more than fifteen years running experiments on both sides, from search, social and interface optimization to product experimentation led by behavioral goals. He argues that growth must work on the live product and feature development as much as on the marketing side. Growth runs its own experiments on what has shipped, alongside the product teams rather than downstream of them. A conversation for leaders who want experiments that move revenue, not only satisfaction and perceived usability upticks.

    Download one-page PDF

    Takeaways

    1. Why growth that stops at marketing leaves most revenue levers untouched: activation, pricing, adoption and retention live in the product.
    2. How growth and product split the work after launch: product owns discovery and delivery, growth experiments on what is live, each with its own backlog.
    3. How growth gets enough involvement in feature development to run product experiments without becoming a second product team.

    Audience

    CMOs, CROs, CPOs, heads of growth and marketing; B2B, SaaS and growth conferences.

    Lengths

    Fireside
    30–45 min
    Panel
    45–60 min

Bios

For programmes, show notes and introductions.

Short

Andreas Mitschke has spent more than fifteen years running product and growth experiments, a path that took him from UX design through code to growth in product and marketing. His work spans B2C and SaaS companies as well as B2B industrial and deep tech organizations. He is the author of Known Before Built: From Discovery to Decision for Enterprises in Transformation.

Long

Andreas Mitschke has spent more than fifteen years running product and growth experiments. His non-linear venture journey began at 15 with web design services, coding a Windows utility and building a travel experience platform, and took him from UX design to growth, product and marketing. His work spans B2C and SaaS companies as well as B2B industrial and deep tech organizations. He has advised startups and scale-ups through funds including 500 Global and Loyal VC, evaluated and brokered deal flow for venture funds across Europe, New York and Silicon Valley, and helped build Germany’s largest academic deep tech program. Closer to the ground, he coached founders at Mannheim’s startup centers and lectured on entrepreneurship and product discovery at universities in Germany. He is the author of Known Before Built: From Discovery to Decision for Enterprises in Transformation.
